Border Collie Shedding Calendar: When They Blow Their Coat, How Long It Lasts and Owner-Tested De-Shedding Routines

Track heavier shedding by season and adjust your brush-out as the coat changes.

Home›Blog›Shedding calendar

Border Collies can shed all year. Spring and fall are useful times to watch for a heavier drop of loose undercoat, often called blowing coat. Plan for a stretch of several weeks. Your own start date and return to ordinary brush yield give you a more useful calendar than a fixed end date.

Where is your Border Collie in the shed?

Compare the same patch of coat across sessions to get an elapsed-day count and a grooming plan for today.

Which seasons should you mark in the calendar?

Mark your local spring and fall as times to watch the brush. VCA's coat-care guide describes seasonal undercoat loss in clumps in thick double-coated dogs and notes that these heavier cycles often occur toward late spring and late fall. Start looking when your own season turns. A change in what the brush picks up is more useful than a date copied from someone else's calendar.

How long does a Border Collie coat blow last?

A few weeks is a planning window. Veterinarian Berat Çelik describes heavy undercoat release over a few weeks in his guide to shedding in double-coated dogs. That guidance covers double coats generally. The length of the heavier stretch in your Border Collie is best measured from the first change in the brush to the return to its usual yield.

Use the first comparable brush-out with more loose fluff than usual as your start marker. Check again with the same tool on the same patch. The obvious tufts can disappear while the brush still picks up more fluff than it did before the shed. Log that as a taper. When comparable checks settle back to the usual amount, note the date you first saw that return.

If you started tracking halfway through, leave the start date blank or label it as the first time you noticed the change. The planner measures elapsed calendar days between the dates you enter. It treats the first day as zero days elapsed. The actual coat change may have started earlier, and an apparent change after a bath or a switch of brushes can reflect how much loose hair you happened to collect.

Do rough and smooth Border Collies shed differently?

Both coat varieties have an outer coat and a soft, dense undercoat, according to the Border Collie Club of South Australia. The club describes light shedding between seasonal sheds and recommends a quick weekly brush as a starting point for upkeep. A smooth Border Collie can still leave loose undercoat in the brush. On a longer coat, part the hair so you can check what is coming from beneath the surface.

Which owner-tested de-shedding routines are worth trying?

The South Australian breed club shares tools its members regularly use on their Border Collies. Their routines include a maintenance brush-out and a bath followed by blow-drying and brushing with a pin brush. The club advises against de-shedding brushes with cutting blades. Pick a tool that suits the coat you can part and comb comfortably, and judge it against your own observations.

Start with a short brush-out

Choose a spot and a moment when your dog already settles. Start on an easy patch, gently work in the direction the coat lies, and collect the loose hair before moving on. If the brush or comb catches, lift it and look at what caught. Use the next session to check another area. This gives a dog that walks away from the brush a manageable routine and gives you a consistent patch for comparing sessions.

Separate the reference check from the rest of the groom

Check your usual patch before you tackle the heavier coat on the rump or behind the legs. If the whole session has to end early, the reference check still belongs in your log. For a longer coat, part the hair in small sections so you can see where the comb travels. Bring stubborn tangles to a groomer, and use the Border Collie groomer checklist to explain that your priority is a brush-out and preservation of the double coat.

Try a bath and brush-out when you need the extra help

VCA advises checking for burrs and tangles before the bath and describes seasonal bathing as an option for dogs with a heavy undercoat. If you book a groom, ask what the bath and drying session involves and which areas they expect to spend time on. Put the appointment in your shedding notes so that when you check again you can account for hair removed during the groom.

What should you put in your shedding log?

Write down the local season, the date, the patch you checked and how much hair the same brush picked up. Add a note for a bath, a salon visit or a tool change. Use the same labels whenever you repeat the check.

How do you compare one season with the next?

Save the start and return dates together with the observation that triggered each entry. Next season, put your reminder near when the last rise began and confirm by brushing. If you have two dogs, keep separate notes even if you brush them on the same days. A pile of mixed hair tells you about work around the house. It gives you little basis for deciding whose grooming routine to change.

For example, if your check goes from loose tufts to extra fluff, leave your next session in place and mark the change as a taper. If your next check gives the usual amount and the following check agrees, move the brush back into your ordinary care routine. Keep the record even if you never found a distinct peak. A well-kept log can help you decide when to put more time into brushing and when the usual schedule is enough.

Frequently asked questions

Do Border Collies shed in winter and summer?

They can shed during both seasons. The breed club describes routine light shedding as well as heavier seasonal sheds. Use the quieter stretches to learn the brush yield you usually see, then compare future checks against that. Keep an entry for any heavier stretch you observe, even when it falls outside your spring or fall reminder.

Does a smooth Border Collie still blow its coat?

It can. The breed club describes an undercoat beneath both coat varieties. Shorter hair can change what you notice around the house, so check what your own brush picks up. If the dog has a very short outer coat, start with a gentle pass on the body and keep your comparison patch consistent.

Should I brush every day during a heavy shed?

Try shorter, more frequent sessions while loose coat is coming away. VCA notes that daily brushing can pick up loose hair that would otherwise wind up around the home. Work within the amount of handling your dog accepts. Return to your upkeep schedule when comparable checks settle back toward the usual amount.

Can I book a de-shed before I see any tufts?

You can ask a groomer about timing, but try making a brush-yield check first. If the amount is still ordinary, keep your routine and take another look at the next session. If you already have a grooming appointment, tell the groomer what the brush is collecting and where the coat is hardest to comb.

How can I tell if the coat blow is over?

Compare the same patch with the same tool and look for a return to the amount you normally collect. Confirm it at another comparable session. A single lighter brush-out after a full groom gives you less information than repeated checks under the same conditions. Ordinary loose hair can continue after the heavier stretch ends.
